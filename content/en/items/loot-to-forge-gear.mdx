## Choosing Your Weapon: Katana vs. Greatsword

When you use your dungeon **loot to forge gear**, the anvil allows you to craft two primary weapon archetypes: the swift Katana and the devastating Greatsword. Both weapon types fulfill distinct combat roles, largely defined by the active skills bound to your Q key.

Community reports and player experiences suggest that while katanas clear trash mobs rapidly due to swift attack frames, greatswords excel at breaking through high-health dungeon elites. The materials and rarity of the ores you provide determine the secondary traits, skill damage, and stat scaling of the finished blade.

### Weapon Archetype Breakdown

*   **Katana:** Emphasizes high attack velocity, rapid repositioning, and wide-sweeping slash skills. Ideal for players who want to clear clustered enemy groups before taking damage.
*   **Greatsword:** Focuses on heavy, crushing impacts with high single-hit burst damage. Skills often feature forward lunges or ground pounds that stagger tougher monsters.

| Weapon Type | Attack Speed | Skill Playstyle (Q Key) | Best Suited For |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Katana** | Fast | Multi-slash sweeps, dash strikes | Crowd control, low-to-mid floors |
| **Greatsword** | Moderate/Slow | Ground slams, high-damage cleaves | Elite enemies, deep floor bosses |

### The "Four-Hit" Retreat Rule

The most critical skill in dungeon survival is knowing when to pull back. Unlike games with gradual difficulty curves, dungeon levels feature massive Power gaps between floors. 

A reliable community benchmark is the **Four-Hit Rule**: If standard enemies on your current floor require more than four strikes to take down, you have reached your build's limit. Attempting to push past this point risks death, which can strip you of valuable unbanked resources. Whenever your damage output falls below this threshold, hit your return hotkey, head to the forge, and use your fresh **loot to forge gear** that matches the new difficulty baseline.

| Mob Health Indicator | Combat Action | Recommended Next Step |

## Power Progression: Auto-Training and Rebirths

Forging elite equipment is your primary damage driver, but character training and the Rebirth system provide the permanent multipliers needed to reach endgame tiers. 

### Active vs. Passive Power Training

When resting between dungeon descents, you should never remain idle. You can increase your baseline Power manually by swinging your blade with the Left Mouse Button inside the hub. Alternatively, you can step into the dedicated auto-training zone to accumulate Power passively while away from the keyboard. While training gains scale slower than crafting upgrades, they establish the foundation required to unlock your next weapon tier.

### The Rebirth Multiplier

Once your Power hits specific thresholds, visit the Rebirth menu. Undergoing a Rebirth temporarily resets your active Power score to zero, but grants permanent, compounding multipliers to future Power gains and Coin acquisition rates.

| Rebirth Stage | Power Threshold | Power Multiplier | Coin Drop Bonus |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Rebirth 1** | 10,000 Power | +50% | +25% |
| **Rebirth 2** | 50,000 Power | +100% | +50% |
| **Rebirth 3** | 250,000 Power | +200% | +100% |
| **Rebirth 4** | 1,000,000 Power | +400% | +200% |

Because your Rebirth status directly boosts coin yields, it drastically accelerates how fast you can buy bag upgrades and enchant high-level gear. Always execute a Rebirth as soon as you meet the requirements; player experience shows that the temporary stat reset is overcome within minutes thanks to your upgraded multipliers.

To maximize your efficiency during your first few hours of play, follow this structured roadmap:


1.  **Initial Power Grind:** Spawn into the hub and auto-train until you reach roughly 500 base Power.
2.  **First Gathering Run:** Enter the dungeon, clear floors one and two, and gather common ores until your starter pouch is full.

6.  **Enchant and Rebirth:** Push toward floor four, gather elite ores, enchant your primary weapon, and trigger your first Rebirth the moment you reach the required threshold.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What happens if I die inside the dungeon?
Dying in the dungeon ends your expedition immediately and sends you back to the hub. In most game modes, any unbanked ores and materials gathered on that run are permanently lost. To protect your progress, always pay attention to enemy health bars and retreat before taking lethal damage.

### Can I choose whether I forge a Helmet or Chest Armor?
No. While you can select the general Armor category at the forge, whether you receive a Helmet or Chest Armor is determined by RNG. Both items provide substantial boosts to your total Power, so crafting multiple times until you complete a full set is standard progression practice.

### How do I acquire more Coins to upgrade my bag?
You earn Coins primarily by selling excess dungeon drops and scrap materials to the hub merchant. After you gather enough high-tier **loot to forge gear**, sell all lower-tier duplicate ores to keep your gold reserves high for inventory expansions and weapon enchantments.

### Should I prioritize weapon forging or auto-training?
Weapon forging provides immediate, exponential leaps in combat capability, whereas training offers steady, linear gains. For active play, forging new weapons using dungeon materials should always be your top priority. Reserve auto-training for breaks or when you are close to hitting a Rebirth milestone.
